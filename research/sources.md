# Source and Rights Research

This is a research roster, not a license to reproduce text. Presence Cards is
open-source software, but every quotation still needs its own rights review.

## Release rule

Use an exact quotation in a published deck only when one of these is true:

1. The specific edition or translation is confirmed public domain in the
   territories where the app is distributed.
2. The project has written permission or a license that covers this use.
3. The source itself grants reuse under terms that clearly cover the card.

Otherwise, write an original Presence Cards practice prompt and list the work
as **suggested reading**. Do not label an original prompt as a quotation or
make it sound as though an author approved it.

Keep a record for every published card: author, work, edition or translation,
link or bibliographic citation, rights basis, verification date, and any
permission record. Attribution is essential, but it does not by itself grant
permission to reproduce copyrighted text.

## Product content principle

Cards must stand on their own before they point elsewhere. Presence Cards'
primary content is original, immediately useful practice language. Exact
public-domain quotations are occasional, clearly attributed guests. A modern
author or work can appear as optional **Further reading** for an original
practice card; it is a route to discovery, not an authority the person must
consult to use the card.

As an initial editorial guide, aim for roughly 70–80% original practices and
20–30% verified quotations across the library. Preserve a card's distinction:
an original practice names Presence Cards as author, while a quotation names
its source, edition/translation, and rights basis.

## Editorial balance

- Aim for at least three distinct source voices in a full deck.
- Use at most one card from a single author in a deck unless the deck is
  explicitly presented as a single-author collection.
- Offer traditions as lenses, never as assumptions a person must share.
- Name the tradition and context when it matters. Do not flatten Buddhist,
  Christian, psychological, or liberation-centered work into generic wellness
  slogans.
- Do not use sacred texts, trauma-focused material, or a marginalized
  community's teachings as decorative content.

## Current-source audit

The existing Pema Chodron, Thich Nhat Hanh, Susan David, Tara Brach, Brene
Brown, and Viktor Frankl quotations should be treated as **permission
required** until a rights review says otherwise. Marcus Aurelius may be public
domain, but a specific modern English translation can still be copyrighted.

The King James Bible is a useful public-domain starting point in some
jurisdictions, but it should not become the app's default voice. Verify the
translation and territory before release.

## Public-domain and link-first prospects

These are promising **research leads**, not blanket clearance. A work's age,
an online transcript, or a claim on a fan/archive site is not enough on its
own: record the exact edition or translation and its rights statement before
placing an exact quotation in a deck.

### Neville Goddard

Neville Goddard is a potentially interesting **link-first** voice for
imagination, possibility, and beginning again. His material, however, should
not be treated as automatically cleared just because it is freely posted.
Several archives assert that some of his early lectures and writings are
public domain, but that is not a primary rights determination and the status
can differ by work, publication date, edition, and territory. For now:

- Add a resource link to an original work, not a quotation card.
- If selecting a specific passage later, record its original publication date
  and validate the exact text against a reliable public-domain source.
- Do not copy an archive's introductions, summaries, formatting, or
  categorization; those additions can be copyrighted even when a source text
  is not.

Useful discovery links: [Neville Goddard bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neville_Goddard), [Read Neville Goddard](https://readnevillegoddard.com/about/), and [Cool Wisdom Books' rights policy](https://coolwisdombooks.com/copyright-use-policy/).

## Next step

Before adding a modern author's words to a deck, find an official reuse policy
or contact the author, publisher, or rights holder. Until then, build original
practice cards that cite the work in a "Further reading" area.
