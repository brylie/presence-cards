# Presence Cards — public-domain source research brief

## Assignment

Build a reviewable source library of **50–100 short, exact quotations** for
Presence Cards. The library will supply several 52-card decks over time, not
fill every deck at once. Prioritize sources that are genuinely reusable in the
United States and can be linked to a stable, reader-friendly source page.

Presence Cards is a gentle reflection app, not a mood tracker, a self-help
program, or a clinical tool. It helps someone meet a moment with attention,
kindness, and agency. The cards should invite presence rather than promise
control, certainty, healing, prosperity, or a cure.

## Non-negotiable rights rule

Only propose an exact quotation when the **specific wording and specific
edition/translation** are clearly one of the following:

1. Marked public domain in the United States by a reliable provider (prefer
   the individual Project Gutenberg book page, Library of Congress item, a
   government rights statement, or a reputable institutional collection).
2. Under an explicit license that permits this form of reuse, with the license
   linked and recorded.
3. Confirmed by written permission held by the project.

Do not infer permission merely because text is old, free online, quoted by
Wikiquote, or attributed to a deceased author. Ancient works and classical
authors need a checked translation. Do not reuse any editor's introduction,
summary, selection, heading, metadata, or transcription unless its rights
also allow reuse.

When rights are unclear, include it only in the **link-only inspiration
register**—never as a quote recommendation.

## Decks and editorial terrain

Collect a balanced pool across these deck areas:

| Deck | What the cards can hold |
| --- | --- |
| Uncertainty | not knowing, openness, taking the next small step |
| Anxiety | steadiness, perspective, a return to the present; never reassurance rituals |
| Anger | dignity, boundaries, non-harm, a pause before action |
| Sadness | tenderness and company without forced optimism |
| Fear | courage alongside fear, wise caution, grounding |
| Waiting | patience, ripening, listening, time |
| Loss | grief, love, remembering, continuing without minimizing loss |
| Change | transition, adaptation, release, agency |
| Enoughness | simplicity, gratitude, self-worth beyond achievement |
| Beginning Again | repair, renewal, a forgiving next step |
| Rest | silence, Sabbath, stillness, limits, unearned rest |

Avoid fatalism, spiritual bypassing, shame, coercive religious language, and
claims that an emotion can be banished by thought. Include a variety of
traditions and avoid treating any tradition as generic wellness content.

## Priority source families

Start with a diverse mix, favoring primary texts or established public-domain
editions:

- Early Quaker writings: George Fox, John Woolman, Margaret Fell, William
  Penn, Isaac Penington, James Nayler. These are especially valuable for
  Inner Light, simplicity, waiting, and worshipful stillness.
- Classical Stoic works: Marcus Aurelius and Epictetus, using a specifically
  cleared English translation.
- Public-domain sacred texts and translations, treated with care and accurate
  citation—never as decoration or a default voice.
- Classical poetry and wisdom traditions only where the particular English
  translation is verified public domain (for example, Rumi, Laozi, Buddhist
  texts, or others).
- Public-domain poets, essayists, and nature writers whose text genuinely
  fits the app's tone.
- U.S. federal-government works only when they are authentically relevant and
  plainly public domain as works of U.S. government employees.

Project Gutenberg is a primary discovery path because individual ebook pages
state U.S. copyright status. It remains necessary to record the exact ebook
and edition used. Also consider Library of Congress, Wikisource only when its
edition is independently verified, and institutional archives with clear
rights statements.

## Contemporary link-only inspiration register

In parallel, build a separate list of 15–30 contemporary works or authors for
future reading links. Do **not** copy their wording unless rights have been
separately documented. For each, create a one-sentence original Presence
Cards practice idea inspired by the *theme*, not by the author’s phrasing.

Starting voices may include Jon Kabat-Zinn, Kristin Neff, Tara Brach, Kaira
Jewel Lingo, Tricia Hersey, Megan Devine, Resmaa Menakem, and Thomas Kelly.
Neville Goddard may be included as a research lead, but each work/edition
needs its own rights verification; freely available archives are not proof of
reuse permission.

## Required deliverables

### 1. Verified quotation library (50–100 entries)

Return a Markdown table or CSV with one row per candidate:

| Field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| ID | Stable slug, e.g. `waiting-woolman-01` |
| Deck fit | One primary deck; optional secondary deck |
| Exact quotation | Keep it brief: normally 8–35 words; preserve punctuation and spelling from the edition |
| Author | Name as shown by the source |
| Work | Full title |
| Edition / translator | Required whenever applicable |
| Precise location | Chapter, section, verse, page, or line when available |
| Source URL | Direct link to the exact online edition/text |
| Reader link | Friendly link to the work or author for in-app “Read more” |
| Rights basis | `public-domain`, `licensed`, or `permission-held` only |
| Rights evidence URL | Direct page that establishes rights or license |
| Territory | At least `United States`; flag any uncertainty elsewhere |
| Verification date | ISO date |
| Context note | 1–2 sentences on meaning, tradition, and any important caveat |
| Card framing | A concise original practice/title that does not impersonate the author |
| Confidence | High / medium; no low-confidence quote enters the verified library |

### 2. Link-only inspiration register (15–30 entries)

For contemporary protected works, return:

| Author | Work or official article | Official/reader URL | Deck fit | Original card idea | Rights note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

The original card idea must be genuinely new wording. Label it
`original-practice`; identify Presence Cards as its author; put the cited work
in “Further reading.”

### 3. Exclusions and questions log

List every appealing quote rejected or held back because the edition,
translation, attribution, licensing, or context was uncertain. Explain what
would be needed to clear it. This is valuable research, not failure.

### 4. Deck coverage summary

Show counts by deck and source voice. Aim for no more than 25% of the verified
library from one author or tradition. Flag any deck with fewer than 5 strong
candidates.

## Quality checks before delivery

- Open every source and rights-evidence link.
- Confirm quotation text against the linked edition; do not rely on quote
  aggregators.
- Distinguish the original author from the translator/editor.
- Verify that each exact quote supports the proposed deck without changing its
  meaning through truncation.
- Prefer a small, strong, well-documented library over filling a quota.
- Do not provide legal conclusions beyond the documented rights evidence;
  flag territorial or publication-history ambiguity for review.

## Output order

1. One-paragraph methodology and scope.
2. Verified quotation library.
3. Link-only inspiration register.
4. Exclusions/questions log.
5. Deck coverage summary and recommended first 12 cards to prototype.
